Key Findings

What the data says at the highest level

Finding 1 · Access dominates

81% of records touch Contact/Access — finding a family doctor is the central concern

Of 56,907 records, 46,438 (81.6%) include at least one Contact/Access term. The remaining 19% address Comprehensiveness (15%), Coordination (8%), or Continuity (7%). The distribution reflects a system where the primary problem is getting in the door.

Finding 2 · Cost & Walk-in tie at #1 topic

37% of records discuss cost or billing; 37% discuss walk-in or urgent care

These are the two largest thematic clusters — equal in size and deeply linked. Walk-in visits are often the entry point to private or out-of-pocket cost. 29% discuss access and waitlists directly; 23% discuss system-level reform.

Finding 3 · Sentiment improved in 2025

Average compound sentiment rose from −0.203 (2024) to +0.274 in 2025

After peaking in negativity in late 2022 (avg compound 0.160 in October), the discourse has slowly improved. April and May 2025 show the lowest negativity percentages of the entire 55-month dataset: 27.0% and 25.7% respectively.

Geographic Key Finding

Nova Scotia at 373/100k — driven by an active Halifax healthcare subreddit

Halifax contributes 2,613 records — 71.5% of all Nova Scotia records. This high concentration reflects an active local subreddit, not necessarily worse health outcomes. Ontario's 98/100k is close to national proportionality. Saskatchewan (37/100k) and especially New Brunswick (near-zero) are structurally excluded from the online discourse, even though both provinces face serious primary care access challenges.

Term-Level Sentiment

How different primary care terms compare — avg compound score

"Primary care" as an abstract concept generates far more positive discourse (+0.531) than "family doctor" as a personal need (+0.180). Walk-in terms are the most negative — they appear in posts about access failure.

Sentiment Key Finding

The word "Primary Care" is 3× more positive than "Family Doctor" (+0.531 vs +0.180)

This replicates the finding from the earlier dataset. When people write about "primary care" abstractly, they are discussing reform, policy, and solutions — more hopeful discourse. When they write about "my family doctor," they are describing a specific personal gap — less hopeful. Walk-in standalone terms have the lowest scores (avg +0.160) — they appear almost exclusively in posts about access failure and long waits.

2025 Signal

April (27.0%) and May (25.7%) 2025 are the lowest-negativity months in 55 months

These represent a genuine improvement relative to the 2022–2024 plateau of 30–35% negativity. Whether this reflects real-world improvements (NP scope expansion, new graduates entering practice) or seasonal/cyclical patterns will be clearer once autumn 2025 data is available. The compound score recovery from +0.177 (May 2024) to +0.310 (May 2025) is the largest year-over-year improvement in the dataset.

Method. VADER (Hutto & Gilbert, 2014) applied per record. Compound score = normalized weighted sum of valence scores from the VADER lexicon. Records with compound ≥ +0.05 classified Positive; ≤ −0.05 Negative; between: Neutral. Term sentiment calculated by averaging compound scores across all records containing that term. Key Topic Insights

What these themes reveal

Cost as a primary theme

37% mention cost — fee-based workarounds have become normalized

Cost and billing appear in 21,519 records — tied for first with walk-in care. Private clinics, virtual services with fees, and file-transfer charges are not edge cases in this dataset: they are the majority experience described when Canadians try to navigate a system nominally built on universal coverage.

Mental Health as access problem

13% discuss mental health — almost always as an access failure, not a clinical discussion

Of 7,400 mental health records, the dominant framing is not "I got help" but "I cannot access help." The topic co-occurs heavily with Access/Waitlists (both present in thousands of records). ADHD and anxiety are the most-cited conditions, consistently paired with referral barriers and out-of-pocket assessment costs.

NP as solution signal

4,363 records mention Nurse Practitioners — and they trend more positive than family doctor records

NP records average +0.344 compound sentiment vs +0.180 for family doctor records. The NP keyword consistently appears in posts where someone has found a workable alternative — a solution rather than a problem. This is the clearest positive signal in the dataset for a specific care model.

Method. Topic assignment by keyword matching across full record text (case-insensitive). Each record assigned to all matching topics — not mutually exclusive. Percentages are share of 56,907 total records containing at least one topic keyword. Co-occurrence means both topic keyword sets appear in the same record.

Framework Interpretation

A system where 81% of online discourse is about getting in, not getting care

The 81% Contact/Access share is the defining feature of this dataset. In a well-functioning primary care system, Comprehensiveness and Continuity would be proportionally larger — patients would be talking about the quality and range of care they receive, not just whether they can access it at all. The 4C distribution here is a structural diagnostic: the system's primary failure is at the gate.

Data Source · Collection · Analysis · Limitations

About & Method

Full methodological disclosure for the v3 dataset. How records were collected, how geography was inferred, how sentiment was measured, and what the data cannot tell us.

Dataset

reddit_pc-direct_v3_2026-07-22.csv

Source and Scope

Platform: Reddit (100% of records). Language: English only (100%). Date range: January 1, 2021 – August 30, 2025. Total records: 56,907 (28,133 top-level posts + 28,774 comments). Known gap: September 2022 is absent from the source file — no records for that month in the dataset.

Collection query: primary_care_keywords_v2 — a keyword set targeting primary care terms including family_doctor, walk_in_clinic, primary_care, nurse_practitioner, and 25+ additional terms. Records were retrieved April 8, 2026.

Geographic Inference

Strong signal (36,630 records, 64.4%): geo_inferred_level = subreddit. Location inferred from subreddit membership (e.g., r/Halifax → Canada-halifax → Nova Scotia). Classified as strong_subreddit.

Weak signal (20,277 records, 35.6%): geo_inferred_level = weak. Location inferred from text mentions of Canadian place names or health system references. Classified as weak_canada_confirmed. These records are tagged Canada-unknown and excluded from province-level analysis.

Limitation: Subreddit membership does not confirm current residence. A person living in Ontario may post in r/Halifax. City-level figures reflect subreddit activity patterns, not verified geographic health utilisation data.

Sentiment Analysis — VADER

VADER (Valence Aware Dictionary and sEntiment Reasoner; Hutto & Gilbert, 2014) was applied to every record's full text. VADER is a rule-based lexicon designed specifically for social media text — it handles informal language, slang, capitalization emphasis, and punctuation signals.

Compound score: Normalized weighted composite of positive, negative, and neutral valence scores. Range: −1.0 (most negative) to +1.0 (most positive). Classification threshold: compound ≥ +0.05 → Positive; ≤ −0.05 → Negative; between → Neutral.

Limitation: VADER was built primarily on English social media text and product reviews. It may underperform on highly technical medical language, sarcasm, or culturally-specific Canadian idioms. It does not capture inter-sentence contradictions in long posts.

Term Classification (pc_direct_terms)

Each record includes a pc_direct_terms field — a pipe-separated list of primary care terms matched in the record text. These were assigned at collection time using the primary_care_keywords_v2 query. The top terms by frequency: family_doctor (36,615 records, 64.3%), walk_in_clinic (9,550), primary_care (7,628), nurse_practitioner (3,359).

One record may match multiple terms. Term-level sentiment averages are calculated across all records containing that term.

4C Framework Classification

Starfield's four dimensions of primary care — Contact/Access, Comprehensiveness, Coordination, Continuity — were used as a post-hoc classification framework. Records were assigned to pillars based on which pc_direct_terms tags they contained. The classification is indicative, not clinically validated. A record tagged family_doctor is classified as Contact/Access because finding a family doctor is the quintessential access problem, not because the record necessarily discusses a successful encounter.

Topic Modeling

Ten thematic clusters were defined by keyword lists applied to full record text (case-insensitive substring matching). This is a dictionary-based approach, not a probabilistic topic model (e.g., LDA). The advantage: interpretable, transparent, reproducible. The limitation: keyword lists may miss emerging terminology and may over-assign records with common words (e.g., "pay" → Cost/Billing, even in neutral contexts).

What This Data Cannot Tell Us

It cannot count unique people. 56,907 records ≠ 56,907 individuals. One active poster may contribute dozens of records. The dataset counts posts and comments, not patients.

It cannot represent all Canadians. Reddit users skew younger, urban, and English-speaking. Populations with lower internet access, lower Reddit participation, or non-English primary language (including many rural, Indigenous, and recent-immigrant communities) are structurally under-represented.

It cannot establish causation. A negative post about walk-in clinics does not mean walk-in clinics are failing — it may mean they are the only option and therefore where frustration concentrates. Sentiment reflects emotional experience, not clinical outcomes.

It cannot determine clinical accuracy. Reported waits, costs, and experiences are self-reported and may not reflect administrative or clinical reality.
